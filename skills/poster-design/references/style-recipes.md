## Direction selection

Choose a recipe for its communication behavior, then vary it across independent axes. Do not equate a recipe with a fixed palette or template.

| Recipe | Choose when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial campaign | Product, launch, promotion, or brand clarity leads | The user wants intimate, raw, or anti-commercial expression |
| Promotional flyer | One-page promotion or event communication needs more approved information than a poster | The deliverable is multipage, a banner, an infographic, or manufacturing-ready artwork |
| Fashion commerce | Model, apparel, shoes, bags, or cross-border commerce leads | Exact catalog preservation requires a product-production workflow |
| Editorial minimal | One idea benefits from restraint and negative space | Dense facts or multiple required assets must be shown |
| Zine print | Cultural, poetic, music, art, or independent editorial expression leads | Glossy commercial polish is required |
| Fine bitmap | The concept benefits from digital-print tension and a graphic subject | Retro game art or smooth photography is expected |
| Photo-led | A supplied or generated photograph is the evidence and emotional anchor | The poster should be primarily typographic or abstract |
| Typography-led | The title itself is the hero and copy is short | Exact long-form body copy is required in-model |
| Graphic abstraction | An abstract message needs a symbol, shape system, or optical event | Literal product or identity fidelity dominates |
| Mixed-media collage | Multiple evidence fragments intentionally create meaning | A clean single-product hierarchy is required |

## Commercial campaign

- Start with the selling idea, then invent one physical or graphic event that demonstrates it. Do not use generic “premium product on a clean background” as the concept.
- Let the product interact with the visual device and title. Use an extreme crop, directional force, scale break, impossible balance, material transformation, or strong graphic interruption when it serves the idea.
- Make the title participate—pressed by, wrapped around, cut through, supporting, or counterbalancing the product—rather than placing it in an empty corner by default.
- Choose a distinctive brand-relevant color/material system. White and light-gray minimalism is only one option, not the commercial default.
- Preserve the exact product separately from the campaign world. Do not preserve generic or unwanted reference-image text as if it were approved poster copy.
- Reject a direction if removing the product would leave a generic gradient, blob, ribbon, arch, or floating-shape template.

## Promotional flyer

- Build one scan path: hook or offer first, subject second, essential details third, CTA/contact last. Do not give every block equal visual weight.
- Preserve supplied products, people, logos, and approved brand elements. Use actual source files rather than describing them back into existence.
- Keep generated typography to the shortest hierarchy. For dates, addresses, prices, terms, QR codes, contact details, or longer copy, reserve deliberate zones and finish with deterministic typesetting.
- For print, design to the requested trim and orientation, but do not call the generated raster print-ready until bleed, safe margin, resolution, color profile, and export format are verified downstream.
- Avoid generic marketing templates, fake QR codes, invented discounts, random badges, illegible filler copy, decorative CTA buttons, and equal-weight content boxes.

## Fashion commerce

Use the cleaned WeShop seed as a starting point only for this recipe: a young female model, cross-border ecommerce, 3:4 large half-body framing, and luxury shoes/bags promotion. Replace every supplied variable and do not assume gender, age, product category, or market when the user specifies otherwise.

- Composition: editorial portrait, dynamic product crop, model-product dialogue, or clean campaign lockup.
- Fidelity: preserve supplied face, garment, accessory, and product design.
- Avoid: generic marketplace template, extra merchandise, invented brand copy, and beauty retouch that changes identity.

## Editorial minimal

- Composition: 60–90% intentional negative space; one small but legible visual event or one large quiet form.
- Typography: serif, mono, typewriter, or restrained sans; title may be small if the subject carries the page.
- Color: neutral field with one purposeful accent.
- Material: clean paper, subtle print defect, restrained photographic or illustrative carrier.
- Avoid: empty-for-empty's-sake composition; the focal carrier must remain visible at thumbnail size.

## Zine print

- Composition: off-center subject, irregular rhythm, cropped or assembled paper events.
- Typography: vernacular type, stamped labels, editorial notes, or one expressive title.
- Color: one or two inks, localized high-chroma accent, or photocopy monochrome.
- Material: xerox, risograph, halftone, letterpress, torn paper, tape, overprint, or scan artifacts.
- Avoid: dense scrapbook defaults, copied reference wording, fake long text, glossy 3D, or cinematic polish.

## Fine bitmap

- Composition: one dominant dot-matrix subject or close subject-title pairing.
- Typography: refined display serif or restrained microtype; readable phrases appear once.
- Color: monochrome or duotone ink with tonal change through dot density.
- Material: fine square pixels, stepped edges, halftone fields, missing-pixel gaps, scan lines, and slight registration error.
- Avoid: retro-game scenes, chunky 8-bit sprites, UI, neon cyberpunk, stickers, or smooth photorealistic detail.

## Photo-led

- Composition: choose one evidence-rich crop; reserve typography space through framing rather than covering the subject.
- Typography: integrate with perspective, shadow, edge, or negative space while protecting faces and products.
- Color: derive from the photograph, then introduce one controlled contrast if needed.
- Material: documentary, flash, cinematic, archival, or clean campaign photography as the brief requires.
- Decide explicitly whether to preserve literal composition or distill the photo's semantic core.

## Typography-led

- Composition: type is the dominant image; subject becomes punctuation, counterform, crop, texture, or secondary anchor.
- Typography: one custom-feeling display behavior—stretched, stacked, wrapped, fragmented, oversized, modular, or spatial—plus at most one support tier.
- Color: use contrast to establish reading order, not decorative variety.
- Avoid: generic centered headline over background art, repeated accidental words, and long copy blocks.

## Graphic abstraction

- Composition: reduce the message to one symbol, shape tension, optical system, or spatial contradiction.
- Typography: precise and subordinate unless type is part of the abstraction.
- Color: monochrome, duotone, complementary tension, or one high-chroma field.
- Material: flat vector-like fields, ink shapes, cut paper, gradients only when conceptually necessary, or optical print effects.
- Avoid: meaningless blobs, floating chrome objects, generic futurism, and decorative geometry without semantic work.

## Mixed-media collage

- Composition: define one anchor and a controlled overlap order; every fragment must support the message.
- Typography: use labels, cut type, handwriting, or a clean counterpoint, but keep required copy legible.
- Color: unify heterogeneous sources through one ink, paper, or grading logic.
- Material: photos, scans, handwriting, tickets, maps, paint, tape, torn edges, or found textures.
- Avoid: random scrapbook density, source duplication, invented documents, and unlicensed brand marks.

## Recipe expansion rule

Add a recipe only when it introduces a meaningfully different construction system, not merely a new palette or named aesthetic. Record:

1. communication use case;
2. composition behavior;
3. subject-to-type relationship;
4. color logic;
5. visible material process;
6. failure modes;
7. at least two eval briefs that should produce visibly different results.

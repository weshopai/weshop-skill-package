# Input and acceptance

## Preflight

- Prefer one isolated, sharp product with the complete silhouette visible.
- Identify product anchors before generation: outer shape, proportions, component count, openings, seams, controls, materials, colors, label/logo positions, and visible text.
- Flag transparent glass/plastic, reflective metal, fine mesh, fur, translucent edges, holes, cables, and low product-background contrast as segmentation risks.
- A single view does not prove hidden surfaces, the back, interior, included accessories, dimensions, capacity, or functional claims.
- Multiple products or a reference board require explicit source roles and usually a multi-reference model.

## Acceptance gate

1. Product silhouette, proportions, perspective, and count match the source.
2. Components, openings, controls, fasteners, seams, packaging structure, label/logo positions, and visible text do not drift.
3. Materials, colors, transparency, gloss, grain, texture, and reflections remain credible and product-faithful.
4. Product edges are clean with no old-background residue, halo, missing thin part, or filled opening.
5. Surface contact, weight, shadow direction, reflections, scale, depth, and occlusion integrate the product into the new scene.
6. Requested scene, surface, mood, and copy space are visibly present without an invented accessory, extra product, person, text, watermark, or collage.
7. Every background object declared mandatory is present. If exact background pixels were requested, compare composition and objects against the accepted background and fail a semantic reinterpretation.
8. Output count and any downstream ratio handling match the delivery plan.

Fail the image when it is attractive but changes a commercially identifying product detail or ignores the requested scene class.
